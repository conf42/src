    Abstract

    Self-service was built around a simple assumption: someone already knows what they want the platform to do. AI breaks that assumption. An agent may detect degradation, decide a deployment caused it, and choose to roll it back—without anyone requesting the action. At that point, the platform is no longer just validating whether an action can be executed safely. It is being asked to trust the decision that the action should happen at all. That changes the platform contract. This session explores the shift from validating execution to validating intent. We’ll examine the boundary between an agent’s decision, the authority it receives, and the action the platform ultimately allows—using operational scenarios where autonomous systems can diagnose, remediate, or change production environments. The goal is not another layer of AI guardrails. It is to rethink what self-service becomes when the consumer of the platform can create its own intent. Self-service asks: “Can I do this?” Safe autonomy adds the harder question: “Should I be allowed to decide that this needs to be done?”
