## Abstract

AI-assisted test execution marks a shift from deterministic, script-driven automation toward adaptive quality engineering systems designed for complex enterprise environments. While regression testing research often validates techniques on small or mid-sized systems, evidence at true enterprise scale remains limited. This session presents AI-assisted test execution as an augmentation layer that strengthens existing Go-based testing frameworks rather than replacing them.  AI test agents combine perception capabilities, learning-based decision engines, and context-aware execution to interpret natural language requirements, navigate evolving interfaces, and validate system behavior under changing conditions. Empirical evaluations across enterprise web systems show that context-driven identification techniques remain resilient even when application structure, attributes, or content change conditions that commonly break traditional automation.  Unlike deterministic automation common in many Go testing pipelines, AI-driven systems operate probabilistically. This requires defined accuracy thresholds, governance controls, and structured human oversight. Research on autonomous systems testing shows that many approaches lack rigorous quantitative validation, making explainability, confidence-based escalation, and fallback strategies essential for enterprise adoption—particularly in regulated environments demanding traceability and auditability.  The session also explores how adaptive execution and learning-based defect prediction can reduce maintenance overhead while improving test effectiveness. Attendees will gain a research-grounded, practical framework for responsibly introducing AI-assisted execution into modern Go-centric engineering ecosystems balancing efficiency, scalability, and trust.
