# Your Rollback Is Untested and You Will Find Out at 3am

A talk at [Conf42 MLOps 2026](https://www.conf42.com/mlops2026) by [Artemis Leonardou](https://www.conf42.com/speaker_Artemis_Leonardou) (Software Engineer & DevRel @ NOFire AI). Page: https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2026_Artemis_Leonardou_rollback_untested_3am

## Abstract

Most teams treat rollback as the safety net that makes everything else acceptable. Ship fast, we can always revert. I believed that too, until the night I tried to revert an automated deployment and found that the revert did not work either.  The change had been written by an agent. It touched more than I thought, in ways that were individually reasonable, and undoing it turned out to be a careful manual unwind rather than one command. That is a bad thing to learn while a service is down, and it is the specific reason I stopped accepting "we can always revert" as a reason to let anything automated ship something unbounded.  Since then I have treated the rollback path as something that has to be tested on purpose, the same way you would test anything else you depend on in an emergency. This talk is how I do that. How I bound a change so reverting it is genuinely one step. How I verify the revert actually runs instead of assuming it will. And what I found once I started deliberately breaking things to see whether recovery behaved the way I expected it to.  I will be honest about the cost, because this slows you down. The useful question is not whether to do it but which changes deserve it, and I will tell you where I draw that line and why.  For anyone whose pipeline has a revert path nobody has ever exercised in anger.
