Your App Is Up. Your Business Is Down.

You've increased your confidence without increasing your coverage, and that's worse because now you trust the dashboard more. There are three specific ways a healthy-looking endpoint checks misses a real failure. The first is the 200 that lies. Your checkout page returns 200 because the page rendered. The page rendering has nothing to do with whether a payment can complete. A status code tells you the server answered. It doesn't tell you the answer was right. The second is the partial failure. Your provider is failing on 12% of requests.

    A check that runs every five minutes has a decent chance of hitting the 80, 80% that work over and reporting all clear while a meaningful slice of your customers can't buy anything. The third is the slow success. Everything completes eventually. Your check has a 30-second timeout, so a request that takes 18 seconds is a pass. Your customer gave up at six. The fix isn't more checks. It's different checks. Instead of asking, "Did the server respond?" You can ask, "Can a customer still complete the thing they came here to do?" That means checking for the actual content you expect on the page, not just the status code. Dependencies hide in predictable places. Script tags and third-party includes in your HTML. Outbound calls in your backend. DNS records, especially CNAMEs pointing at someone else's infrastructure.     Grab your code base for outbound HTTP calls and API keys. Look at your DNS zone. Look at what your app talks to during a real checkout, not just a page load. Payments, authentication, CDN and DNS, email, analytics, AI. For each group, ask two questions. If this degrades, what does a customer experience, and would we know? You cannot rely on a platform to report its own outage, and you shouldn't build your monitoring on the assumption that it will. The reason is structural, not moral. A vendor status page is updated by the vendor. Your provider's status page lives inside the provider.     Abstract

    Your monitoring is green. Your app returns a healthy 200 for every check you configured. And your customers can’t pay you, because Stripe is degraded and your checkout flow silently died twenty minutes ago. This is what most outages actually look like now. The thing that broke isn’t the thing you built. It’s the payment provider, the identity provider, the CDN, the LLM API you added last quarter. Your app is fine. Your app is also useless. I work on monitoring, and this talk is about the gap between “is my service responding” and “does my service actually work.” I’ll go through what dependency-driven failure looks like in practice, why the usual response of adding more checks doesn’t fix it, and how you find out what your app depends on in the first place, including the fact that most teams genuinely don’t know. I’ll also make an argument I’m happy to defend: you can’t trust a platform to report its own outage. The party with the incentive to under-report is the same party doing the reporting, which is why an outside observer still matters no matter how good your internal telemetry gets. Practical takeaways for anyone who owns an on-call rotation and has ever watched a dashboard stay green through an incident.
