# Feature Flags as Incident Evidence: Safer AI-Assisted RCA Workflows

A talk at [Conf42 Feature Flags 2026](https://www.conf42.com/featureflags2026) by [Ishan Shah](https://www.conf42.com/speaker_Ishan_Shah) (Staff Software Engineer @ PayPal). Page: https://www.conf42.com/Feature_Flags_2026_Ishan_Shah_flags_incident_rca

Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gkSmIznZs9o

## Abstract

AI agents can make incident response faster. Without governance, they can also make the wrong response faster.  This talk starts in the middle of a production incident: the alert is clear, but the next safe decision is not. Metrics are in one system, logs in another, feature flags somewhere else, a deployment landed recently, and the runbook may be stale. The on-call engineer is not slow; they are assembling decision context across too many systems under pressure.  Beacon is a governed decision-control plane for AI-assisted incident response. Instead of starting with an open-ended chatbot, Beacon turns an alert into a workflow: evidence intake, ranked hypotheses, explicit operator decision, scoped follow-up, and traceability throughout. The model is a reasoning component over structured facts. It does not own the incident workflow, merge changes, promote deploys, or bypass approval.  We will walk through the architecture patterns that make this boundary credible for teams that use feature flags as release controls, incident evidence, rollback candidates, and policy gates: typed evidence from metrics, logs, feature flags, Kubernetes context, and repository history; service-bounded retrieval for runbooks and architecture notes; confidence as a workflow signal rather than production authority; policy gates before action; and post-incident artifacts that preserve what the agent knew, what policy allowed, and who made the decision.  Attendees will leave with a practical mental model for production-minded AI agents: evidence first, reasoning second, operator decision third, and an audit trail that survives the chat window.
