# Replay-Safe Architecture: Failure Testing Event-Driven Recovery

A talk at [Conf42 Chaos Engineering 2026](https://www.conf42.com/chaos2026) by [Ishan Shah](https://www.conf42.com/speaker_Ishan_Shah) (Staff Software Engineer @ PayPal). Page: https://www.conf42.com/Chaos_Engineering_2026_Ishan_Shah_replay_event_recovery

## Abstract

Kafka is healthy. Consumer lag is zero. Dashboards are green. Then someone finds a bounded gap in downstream business state.  This talk is about the uncomfortable part of chaos engineering for event-driven recovery: proving that recovery repaired the business state instead of just replaying records successfully.  We will walk through a production-shaped incident in an inventory platform where a damaged interval of events forces a series of architectural questions: which range is safe to replay, which identity protects the business effect, where idempotency actually lives, how long dedupe evidence must survive, what happens when historical events meet current policy, and how to keep repair traffic from becoming the next incident.  The core claim is simple: availability tells us the machinery is running; replayability tells us whether we can reconstruct, repair, and trust state after damage. Attendees will leave with a practical recovery checklist for event-driven systems: separate delivery guarantees from business-effect guarantees, name the idempotency and ordering boundaries, preserve enough evidence for audit, bound blast radius with approval and rate controls, and close incidents with reconciliation proof rather than job completion.
