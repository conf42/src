    Abstract

    Most of the safety tooling around agents is built to tell you what happened. The alert fires, the log line lands, someone reads it, and by then the action has already run. That ordering works when a human is taking the action, because humans hesitate. Agents do not. I have been shipping AI-generated code to production since I was fourteen, and the thing that changed my practice most was realising that nearly every check I had was on the wrong side of the action. I learned that expensively, in an incident where my alerting worked exactly as designed and was still useless, because it told me about something that had already broken. This talk is about moving the check earlier. What it actually means to evaluate an action before it executes rather than after. Where you can put that check in a pipeline you already have, rather than in a redesigned one. What it does to your latency and your shipping speed, honestly. And which classes of action are worth gating at all, versus the ones that are cheaper to let fail and revert. I will be specific about the ones I gate and the ones I deliberately do not, because gating everything is its own failure mode. Tool-agnostic, nothing to install, no product content. For anyone whose agents already have write access to something they care about.
