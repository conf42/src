# Feature Flags as a Migration Engine: De-risking Domain-Driven Decomposition of Monolithic Payment Platforms

A talk at [Conf42 Feature Flags 2026](https://www.conf42.com/featureflags2026) by [Jaya Chand Bandi](https://www.conf42.com/speaker_Jaya_Chand_Bandi) (Independent Researcher @ Independent Researcher). Page: https://www.conf42.com/Feature_Flags_2026_Jaya_Chand_Bandi_migration_payment_platforms

Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SYguzfN6Qu8

## Abstract

Enterprise payment platforms have traditionally been built as monolithic systems to provide centralized deployment, dependable transactional behavior, and strong operational control. While these systems have successfully supported years of business growth, merchant onboarding, and product expansion, they become increasingly difficult to evolve as organizations introduce new payment methods, digital wallets, loyalty programs, hosted checkout solutions, and regional compliance requirements. This presentation explores how feature flags enable a safer, incremental path through Domain-Driven Decomposition, allowing teams to modernize these complex platforms around business capabilities rather than technical convenience without exposing the business to all-or-nothing cutover risk.  The session examines how Domain-Driven Design (DDD) defines bounded contexts across payment domains such as authorization, settlement, dispute handling, loyalty, and reporting, and how flags allow teams to route traffic between old and new implementations while these boundaries are validated in production. It discusses how ubiquitous language and context mapping help identify hidden dependencies and domain ownership, with flag-controlled rollouts providing a mechanism to test these boundaries against real transaction volume before committing fully.  The presentation introduces a taxonomy of migration patterns built around feature flagging as the control layer, including the Strangler Fig Pattern, Anti-Corruption Layer, and Branch by Abstraction, the last of which depends directly on toggles to switch between legacy and new code paths during active development. It presents a practical framework for sequencing extraction based on domain clarity, data ownership, operational criticality, and organizational readiness, with flag-based kill switches offering a fast rollback path if a newly extracted service misbehaves under production load.  Finally, the session discusses common challenges such as false decomposition, shared-database dependencies, over-fragmentation, and organizational alignment influenced by Conway's Law, along with the discipline required to manage flag lifecycle and technical debt as migrations progress. Attendees will gain practical insights into using feature flags to plan safer, reversible domain-driven modernization strategies that improve maintainability, service ownership, and long-term architectural evolution in enterprise payment platforms.
