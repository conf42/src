# Adding Tamper-Evident Audit Trails to AI Agents in Production

A talk at [Conf42 MLOps 2026](https://www.conf42.com/mlops2026) by [Joao Marques](https://www.conf42.com/speaker_Joao_Marques) (Founder @ Asqav). Page: https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2026_Joao_Marques_audit_tamper_agents

## Abstract

AI agents are making real decisions in production - calling APIs, writing code, moving data. But most teams have no reliable way to prove what an agent did, when it did it, or whether the output was modified after the fact. When something goes wrong, you are left digging through logs that anyone could have edited.  This talk covers a practical approach to solving that problem: hash-chained, cryptographically signed audit trails that plug into the agent frameworks teams already use - CrewAI, LangChain, Haystack, LiteLLM, and others.  I will walk through how Asqav adds tamper-evident logging to agentic workflows with a few lines of code. Every agent action gets recorded in a hash chain (each entry references the previous one, so you cannot insert or delete entries without breaking the chain) and signed using ML-DSA-65, a post-quantum digital signature algorithm standardized by NIST. The result is an audit trail that is independently verifiable - anyone can check that the record has not been altered.  The talk will focus on real production concerns:  - Why standard logging and observability tools are not enough when you need to prove integrity, not just observe behavior - How hash-chaining and digital signatures work together to create tamper evidence without requiring a blockchain or external service - What it looks like in practice: adding audit trails to a CrewAI agent pipeline in under 10 lines of Python - How this maps to EU AI Act requirements around traceability and record-keeping (Article 12) - Performance overhead, storage considerations, and key management tradeoffs in production deployments - Multi-party approval workflows - requiring human sign-off before an agent executes sensitive actions  This is not a theoretical talk. It is based on a production-ready open-source toolkit (1,700+ tests, pip-installable) that teams are using today. The audience will leave with a clear understanding of how to add verifiable audit trails to their own agent systems and why this matters for compliance, debugging, and accountability.
