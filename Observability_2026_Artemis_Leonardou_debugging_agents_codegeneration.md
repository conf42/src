# Three Hours Debugging Code I Never Wrote

A talk at [Conf42 Observability 2026](https://www.conf42.com/obs2026) by [Artemis Leonardou](https://www.conf42.com/speaker_Artemis_Leonardou) (Software Engineer & DevRel @ NOFire AI). Page: https://www.conf42.com/Observability_2026_Artemis_Leonardou_debugging_agents_codegeneration

Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s8DsnD_H4EY

## Abstract

An agent wrote a change. It passed review, it passed CI, and then it took a production path down for about three hours. Nothing in the diff was wrong on its own, which is exactly why it got through, and the failure only existed in the combination of the edits. What made it slow was not the code. My dashboards told me what was happening and roughly where, and then stopped being useful, because the question I actually needed answered was why the change had been written that way. I could not ask the author, and none of the reasoning existed anywhere. Commit message, pull request description, ticket: nothing. That is a gap in what we record rather than a gap in what we measure, and I do not think our tooling has caught up with it yet. I have been shipping AI-generated code to production since I was fourteen. This talk is that incident in detail, what my telemetry did and did not give me while I was in it, and the three things I changed afterwards. Keeping a change small enough that reverting it is genuinely one step. Testing that the revert works instead of assuming it does. And moving the check that matters to before an action executes rather than alerting after it has already landed. I will be honest about what each one costs in shipping speed, because none of them are free. Tool-agnostic, no live demo. If your team is starting to let agents write the changes you are on call for, this is the failure mode I wish someone had described to me first.
