# Chaos at the Edge Case: Interaction-Only Failures in Safety-Critical Autonomous Systems

A talk at [Conf42 Chaos Engineering 2026](https://www.conf42.com/chaos2026) by [Govardhan Reddy Kothinti](https://www.conf42.com/speaker_Govardhan_Reddy_Kothinti) (Software Design Safety Architect @ General Motors). Page: https://www.conf42.com/Chaos_Engineering_2026_Govardhan_Reddy_Kothinti_autonomous_safety_failures

## Abstract

Most fault-injection campaigns test one failure at a time: kill a sensor, degrade an actuator, see what breaks. But in safety-critical autonomous systems, the scariest failures often live at the edge case where two individually "safe" degradations overlap — and single-fault chaos testing walks right past them.  We ran this experiment on an autonomous emergency braking (AEB) system. A sensing delay, tested alone, was fine. A reduced braking capability, tested alone, was fine. Combined, across 5,544 simulated scenarios, 209 cases turned out hazardous only in combination — invisible to either single-fault test. A follow-up fault-injection pass added realistic brake-actuator lag and re-tested every one of those 209 edge cases: more than half survived the harder model, and the reason the rest didn't is the most useful part of the story.  This talk covers how we defined, located, and prioritized these "interaction-only" failures: a deterministic parameter sweep, a 200,000-run Monte Carlo campaign, and a margin-based boundary analysis simple enough to run against your own failure-mode matrix. We'll also cover the concrete next step — targeted high-fidelity simulation of the worst boundary cases — for teams who need to close the gap between "found in chaos testing" and "validated for production."
