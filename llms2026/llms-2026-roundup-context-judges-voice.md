# Conf42 LLMs 2026 roundup: context, judges and voice agents

Conf42 Blog, 2026-06-19. https://www.conf42.com/llms2026/llms-2026-roundup-context-judges-voice

In short: Conf42 Large Language Models 2026 in review, from agent memory and knowledge graphs to verifiers, voice pipelines under 500 ms and regulated AI.

By June 2026 nobody at [Conf42 Large Language Models 2026](https://www.conf42.com/llms2026) needed convincing that models can write code or answer questions. The 34 talks, streamed online on June 18, 2026, were about everything around the model: what context it sees, who checks its work, how fast it has to answer on a phone call, and how it behaves inside a bank's ledger. Speakers came from Harness, AWS, Revolut, Snowflake, Microsoft, Meta, Walmart, Expedia Group and a good number of startups.

## The bottleneck is context, not capability

The keynote set the tone. [Prateek Mittal](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Prateek_Mittal_knowledge_graphs_delivery), Director of Product Management at Harness, argued that the next limit on AI in software delivery is context, and showed a knowledge graph of services, pipelines, deployments and owners that an agent queried to find the pipelines failing most often and why.

Three other talks attacked the same problem from different angles. [Anannya Roy Chowdhury](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Anannya_Roy_Chowdhury_memory_agents_context) (AWS) observed that agents in production rarely fail because they can't reason; they fail because they retrieve the wrong context. The talk's framework splits agent memory into working, retrieval and persistent layers. [Lior Schejter](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Lior_Schejter_context_agents_reasoning) described what happens when an agent meets 200 microservices instead of one repo: it gets lost in the middle. The fix is an AI-maintained architecture hub of APIs, domain models and events written for agents to read. [Varun Joshi](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Varun_Joshi_rag_metadata) (AWS) made the data engineer's version of the case: your schema, lineage, query history and dbt models are the context, and most teams haven't wired them into RAG yet.

## Who judges the agent?

[Vsevolod Voropaev](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Vsevolod_Voropaev_verification_agents_production) (Olo din) gave one of the sharper talks of the day: the verifier is the hardest component of an agentic system and usually the last one built. Drawing on a bandit-based verification layer routing across Claude, GPT and Gemini, he argued that verifiers should usually be smarter than the agents they watch. [Liliya Imasheva and Michael Banf](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Liliya_Imasheva_Michael_Banf_validation_summaries_evaluation) (Perelyn) showed a summary-validation pipeline that pairs BERTScore, ROUGE and BLEU with LLM-as-judge scoring on completeness, conciseness, harmlessness, correctness and readability.

Permissions were the other half of trust. [Sohan Maheshwar](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Sohan_Maheshwar_deterministic_authorization_agents) (AuthZed) pointed out that letting a coding agent decide for itself what is safe is a probabilistic answer to a question that needs a deterministic one, and showed how to add enforceable permission boundaries. [Neeraj Kumar Singh Beshane](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Neeraj_Kumar_Singh_Beshane_cryptography_compliance_pipelines) (Meta) presented RuntimeGuard-AI, which records tamper-evident, hash-chained evidence across an inference pipeline for EU AI Act compliance, and [Arunkumar Muthuramalingam](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Arunkumar_Muthuramalingam_identity_security_iam) (General Reinsurance) treated IAM as the core of AI security.

## Voice, phones and people who don't want an app

Voice agents got a strong cluster. [Bohdan Snisar](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Bohdan_Snisar_httpsyoutube5XSR13KHev4) (Solea AI) started from the classic STT, LLM, TTS chain, where every stage waits for the last and the whole conversation turn has under 500 milliseconds. He built the pipeline up layer by layer, through realtime audio transport, voice activity detection and turn detection, to show where it hits the wall, then presented an architecture that reasons in parallel while the agent talks. [Pavel Shchekotov](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Pavel_Shchekotov_voice_ai_trust) (Denk Solutions) removed the app altogether and put an LLM agent behind an ordinary phone number for people who are sceptical of AI.

The most British title of the event belonged to [Anton Denisov](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Anton_Denisov_british_sarcasm) (Dwelly), whose system reads emails and calls for dissatisfaction that customers soften with politeness, understatement or silence. Anyone who has heard a British colleague call a plan 'brave' will appreciate the problem. [Andrii Veremiienko](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Andrii_Veremiienko_shipping_ai_features) (Revolut) brought the mobile view: latency, inference costs, rate limits and UX design around answers that aren't deterministic. [Petras Bazdaras](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Petras_Bazdaras_reading_new_writing) argued that when generating code is cheap, reading it is the skill that matters.

## Shipping it: pipelines, modernisation and serving

[Denis Ermakov](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Denis_Ermakov_deployment_cicd_automation) (Tehpotok) presented an open-source framework for agent-driven CI/CD with retries, observability and explicit points where humans stay in the loop. [Marco Antonio Silva](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Marco_Antonio_Silva_modernization_specs_cloud) (Microsoft) shared spec-driven modernisation with Spec2Cloud and the GitHub Copilot Modernization CLI, including a large project at a Portuguese financial institution. [Tim Spann](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Tim_Spann_sql_automation_operations) (Snowflake) showed Cortex Code managing schema changes and deployments from natural language.

On the serving side, [Kosseila Haddalene](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Kosseila_Haddalene_multimodal_inference_vllm) (Cloudthrill) explained why diffusion transformers, which denoise instead of generating tokens and are compute-bound rather than memory-bound, are awkward to serve next to LLMs, and how vLLM-Omni handles both. [Sergey Speranskiy](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Sergey_Speranskiy_kubernetes_infrastructure_llms) covered the Kubernetes assumptions that break first under LLM workloads, [Kandasamy Selvaraj](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Kandasamy_Selvaraj_performance_lag_signatures) (ADP) shifted RAG performance testing left into CI/CD, and [Leo Visser](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Leo_Visser_governance_apis_control) (OGD ICT-diensten) put Azure API Management in front of models for content filtering, token-aware throttling and caching. [Andrei Shcherbinin](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Andrei_Shcherbinin_moderation_safety_platforms) (Social Discovery Group) walked through a production automoderation stack that mixes lightweight classifiers with multimodal LLMs for grey areas.

## LLMs in regulated systems

A large share of the programme dealt with finance, tax, healthcare and audit. [Abdul Basit Iqbal](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Abdul_Basit_Iqbal_compliance_agents_tax) (Amazon) turned changing tax regulations into executable compliance logic, [Karishma Velisetty](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Karishma_Velisetty_governance_audit_assistants) governed AI code assistants in SOX audit analytics, [Ravindra Rajasekhar Kavuru](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Ravindra_Rajasekhar_Kavuru_privacy_compliance_credit) (Expedia Group) built compliance into co-branded credit card apps, and [Rakesh Jha](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Rakesh_Jha_ledgers_finance_accounting) (DXC Technology) applied LLMs to general ledgers. On the people side, [Lakshmi Priya Gopalsamy](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Lakshmi_Priya_Gopalsamy_latency_reasoning_reliability) made the point that as AI speeds up execution, human decision capacity becomes the constraint, and [Susanta Kumar Sahoo](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2026_Susanta_Kumar_Sahoo_copilots_sre_remediation) looked at LLM copilots for incident response.
