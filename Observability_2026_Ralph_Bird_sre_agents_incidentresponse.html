AI Coded It, AI Shipped It, Who Owns It?

Video size:

Abstract

It’s 03:47. The pager fires and you open your terminal. The code that broke was written by an AI agent, shipped by another AI agent, and never seen by a human. The SRE agent has already failed. It’s on you. Are you ready? Coding agents have already changed how software gets built. The next shift is coming for how we run it in production: SRE agents are starting to triage, correlate, and will soon remediate before a human is awake. PagerDuty’s own “Code It, Ship It, Own It” mantra is under real pressure, the “Code It” and “Ship It” parts are increasingly automated, but “Own It”? That hasn’t gone anywhere. We’ve faced this kind of transition before. Civil aviation automated huge parts of flying decades ago, and the answer wasn’t to remove pilots, it was to mandate simulator time, so pilots stay sharp for the moments autopilot can’t handle. On-call engineers need the same: practiced reps in low-stakes scenarios, not hope that expertise survives on its own once agents take the routine work. This talk looks at what “owning” an incident means when you didn’t write the code and an agent already tried and failed to fix it, and lays out what observability and incident tooling needs to provide to make that ownership possible, from helping you to train for the big one, to providing you with support when it happens
