# The CTO DevOps Handbook: Simple Principles and Examples

A talk at [Conf42 DevOps 2024](https://www.conf42.com/devops2024) by [Michael Zion](https://www.conf42.com/speaker_Michael_Zion) (Founder & CEO @ MeteorOps). Page: https://www.conf42.com/DevOps_2024_Michael_Zion_cto_handbook_principles_examples

Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zm3_tonsdeM

## Abstract

Nail the DevOps part as your company's CTO:
- Understand what’s DevOps (in simple words)
- Know what’s possible with DevOps (in simple goals)
- Get simple _when-to-do-what_ DevOps guidelines
###*[Blogpost](https://www.meteorops.com/blog/the-cto-devops-handbook-simple-principles-and-examples)*

## Key takeaways

- Michael Zion is the co founder and CTO at Meteorops, a DevOps services company. This handbook is for anyone that wants to understand, in simple words, what's DevOps. Hopefully by the end of this talk, you'll have a bunch of principles that are useful.

- Many founders don't know what to expect from DevOps. I want to tell you what's possible. And I'll provide you with some simple examples. The final thing is when to do what. This is the principles part. And the part where I give you some use cases.

- When it comes to software engineering, it's computer science. We don't mathematically prove functions, we test them. We treat it as a science, not as a mathematical discipline. So we do something and then we test if it works.

- DevOps has a simple meaning. And DevOps engineers enable developers to operate the system. When I say operate I mean provision. And configure is just a way to modify configurations in your system. Every company chooses the balance between how much the developers should do it.

- The main function of a DevOps engineer is to enable to operate. DevOps engineers enable developers to operate the system and operate some of it themselves. There are three things within this balance that you can tweak. Each company has it differently.

- The next thing is choose the right tools. The tool should have native state management built inside. Second thing, it has a big community and good docs. The more common a tool is, the more likely you are to have solutions or common problems somewhere.

- Set useful goals. What do I mean by set useful goals? I mean you want to do DevOps. Split shared and end specific resources deployment. Don't complicate things, just keep it simple and make it easier for you to work.
