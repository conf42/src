## Abstract

Federal infrastructure, telecommunications, utility, and defense projects operating within historic districts and federally protected landmarks face complex environmental compliance requirements under the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) and Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act (NHPA). Analysts must evaluate regulatory thresholds across multiple federal agencies, often relying on manual review processes that increase permitting timelines and administrative workload.  This session presents a real-world case study of building an AI-powered permitting intelligence platform that automates NEPA Categorical Exclusion (CE) determinations for historically sensitive sites. The talk focuses on the prompt engineering, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and system architecture patterns used to support regulatory decision workflows while maintaining traceability and human oversight.  The platform integrates large language models, computer vision, geospatial intelligence, and regulatory knowledge retrieval across federal and state data sources, including Google Street View, NPS IRMA, FCC ULS, EPA ECHO, USGS 3DEP, and State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) systems. Attendees will see how structured prompts, multi-step reasoning workflows, and evidence-grounded retrieval strategies are used to analyze construction drawings, GIS boundaries, LiDAR data, imagery, and environmental records to evaluate CE thresholds such as ground disturbance, structure height increases, right-of-way constraints, and potential impacts on National Register-listed properties.  The presentation will cover practical implementation lessons, including prompt design for regulatory reasoning, retrieval grounding techniques, multi-source evidence synthesis, documentation generation, auditability, and human-in-the-loop validation. It will also discuss architectural decisions, integration challenges, and workflow automation approaches that reduced environmental data retrieval time and streamlined coordination across multiple agencies.  Attendees will leave with reusable prompt engineering patterns and system design approaches that can be applied to other high-stakes domains where accuracy, explainability, compliance, and operational efficiency are critical.
