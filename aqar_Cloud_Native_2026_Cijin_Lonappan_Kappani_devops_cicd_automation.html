    Abstract

    Last-mile delivery remains one of the most complex and cost intensive layers of modern retail infrastructure. As same-day and sub-hour fulfillment become baseline expectations, traditional driver-based models struggle to scale efficiently in dense urban environments. Retailers are now turning to autonomous delivery systems to modernize fulfillment architecture and improve reliability, cost control, and operational elasticity. This session explores three primary autonomous delivery modalities reshaping enterprise fulfillment: sidewalk delivery robots, unmanned aerial drones, and autonomous middle-mile trucking systems. Sidewalk robots operate on pedestrian infrastructure using zero-emission electric systems. Drones enable direct point-to-point routing that bypasses surface congestion. Autonomous trucks are among the most mature applications, particularly for predictable warehouse-to-store middle-mile routes. Real-world deployments show this shift is already active. Walmart and Gatik operate fully driverless middle-mile routes in Arkansas without safety drivers. Walmart and Wing conduct drone delivery operations in the Dallas metropolitan area targeting fulfillment within 30 minutes of order placement. 7-Eleven and Nuro have piloted autonomous suburban delivery programs. The session also examines emerging multi-modal coordination models, including robot-to-drone handoff systems piloted in Dallas through Serve Robotics and Wing. These integrated transfer and scheduling frameworks illustrate how cloud native orchestration, distributed systems, and real-time coordination enable scalable autonomous logistics. Attendees will leave with a practical framework for evaluating adoption barriers such as regulatory fragmentation, infrastructure constraints, and environmental limitations, and how cloud native architecture supports resilient, scalable autonomous fulfillment networks.
