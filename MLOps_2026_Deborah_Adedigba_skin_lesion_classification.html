    Abstract

    Building a machine learning model that performs well in an experiment is only one part of developing a reliable ML system. Reproducibility, data quality, evaluation methodology and deployment readiness are equally important, particularly in healthcare applications where model predictions could eventually inform clinical decisions.

    In this talk, I will use my research on deep learning for skin lesion classification as a case study to explore the practical considerations involved in developing an end-to-end medical imaging workflow. My work combines U-Net segmentation with CNN architectures, including ResNet50, VGG16 and EfficientNetB4, and achieved a reported AUC of 0.99 on the evaluated PH² dataset.

    I will discuss the challenges of structuring image-processing and classification workflows, maintaining consistency across preprocessing and evaluation, interpreting performance metrics, and recognising the limitations of results obtained from a single dataset. I will also examine what would be needed to move from an experimental research pipeline towards a more reproducible, monitored and deployment-ready ML system, including versioned data and models, automated testing, experiment tracking and independent validation.

    Using this case study, I aim to demonstrate why reliable machine learning requires more than selecting an architecture and achieving a strong metric. It requires a systematic approach to the entire lifecycle, from data preparation and experimentation to validation, monitoring and maintenance.
