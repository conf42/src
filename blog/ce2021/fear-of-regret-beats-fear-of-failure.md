# Fear regret more than failure, says Locelle's founder

Conf42 Blog, 2021-02-25. https://www.conf42.com/blog/ce2021/fear-of-regret-beats-fear-of-failure

In short: Humaira Ahmed (Locelle) at Conf42 Chaos Engineering 2021 on fear of failure vs fear of regret - three personal stories and three lessons for women in tech

A chaos engineering conference spends most of its time on how systems fail. At Conf42 Chaos Engineering 2021, [Humaira Ahmed](https://www.conf42.com/Chaos_Engineering_2021_Humaira_Ahmed_fear_failure), founder and CEO of the mentorship platform Locelle, turned the topic on the people: the fear of failure holds us back, and the fear of regret is the one worth listening to.

This is not a technical talk. It is a short keynote-style story, told in three parts from her own life, with three lessons at the end.

## Two kinds of fear

Ahmed separates them early. The fear of failure is the fear of losing, of rejection, of not being good enough. The fear of regret is different. Humaira Ahmed said at Conf42 Chaos Engineering 2021: "it's the fear of regret that drives us forward, unlike the fear of failure that holds us back."

## Story one: the first engagement

She grew up in Pakistan, in what she describes as a male-dominated society that is often suppressive towards women, and moved to Canada at 20. At 15 she was engaged to a man twelve years older whom she had never seen; he was educated, employed and had money, which is what mattered. She explains that in Pakistan an engagement is treated as seriously as a marriage.

She had dreams and ambitions and saw them disappearing, and the only thing she could do was cry, every night, to her mother. Two months before the wedding she fell ill and was diagnosed with acute depression. Then her fiancé got a US work visa and offered two options: marry in ten days and wait three years with his family, or wait three years and marry later. Her father, who had watched her cry every night, ended the engagement. Her lesson: listen to your body and your gut; when something does not feel right, it probably is not.

## Story two: the second engagement

About a year later she was engaged again, to another man twelve years older. Crying would not be enough this time, so she applied to the best schools and got into one of the best software engineering programmes in Pakistan. The fiancé's family objected: she would lose her light complexion, she would have affairs, and she would not work after marriage anyway. They suggested she learn to cook a different breakfast every day, lose weight and study home economics. Her mother apologised for rushing into it, and her father returned the ring. The lesson: commit to yourself. Crying alone had not worked twice; the hard work to get into that school gave her the leverage.

## Story three: Locelle

Years later, in Canada, married and a mother of two girls, she had the idea for Locelle. People reminded her that only 5% of tech CEOs are women and that over 90% of tech startups fail in their first year. She focused instead on what she would regret not trying, partly to set an example for her daughters. Locelle had just celebrated its third anniversary when she spoke, and she points out that fewer than 30% of companies make it past three years. Locelle works on equitable workplaces for women through one-to-one mentorship matching with industry leaders and on-the-go guidance.

## The three lessons

Challenge the status quo, especially when it is the right thing to do. Persevere for the things that matter. Commit to yourself and show up for yourself, which she notes women in particular are raised not to do. Her closing ask: write down the thing you would regret not going for, and focus on it.

The abstract also lists the power of mentorship and bootstrapping a tech startup as themes; in the recording, both appear inside the third story rather than as sections of their own.
