# Conf42 MLOps 2025 roundup: where the lab meets production

Conf42 Blog, 2025-09-19. https://www.conf42.com/blog/mlops2025/mlops-2025-roundup-lab-meets-production

In short: Conf42 MLOps 2025 in review, from warehouse robots and vendor lockouts to trillion-parameter training, AgentCore, zero trust pipelines and fintech ML.

Two days before Elementor launched its AI Site Planner, OpenAI locked the company's account. Dennis Nerush told that story at [Conf42 MLOps 2025](https://www.conf42.com/mlops2025), and the punchline was that it barely mattered, because the team had built for exactly that failure. The 37 talks at the event, streamed online on September 18, 2025, kept coming back to the same gap: models that work in the lab, in the notebook or in the demo, and what it takes to keep them working once real traffic, real dust and real vendors get involved. Speakers came from Meta, Amazon, AWS, Mastercard, Oracle, Intuit, JPMorgan Chase, Barclays, SiriusXM and many more.

## The real world is messier than the lab

[Shubham Beldar](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Shubham_Beldar_when_ai_automation) (Symbotic) brought the most physical failures of the event. Picking robots that look flawless in the lab meet transparent shrink wrap, irregular products and dust on their vision sensors, and accuracy drops from the mid-nineties to the low sixties. His diagnosis is an MLOps one: training data missing the messy cases, and validation done in the lab instead of on the warehouse floor.

[Dennis Nerush](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Dennis_Nerush_scaling_ai_fault) (Elementor) covered the vendor version of the same lesson. His team's infrastructure integrates several AI vendors and can switch between them, which is why an account lockout two days before launch turned into a manageable incident instead of a war room. [Madhavi Bhairavabhatla](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Madhavi_Bhairavabhatla_fail_fast_recover) (Mastercard) went one level down to timeouts: set too generously, they hold resources, delay failure detection and let one slow service drag down the rest, so the talk lays out cascading timeouts across system layers.

Several talks addressed the lab-to-production pipeline directly, including [Jimmy Katiyar](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Jimmy_Katiyar_model_production_building) (SiriusXM), [Srikanth Vissarapu](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Srikanth_Vissarapu_lab_production_building) (Meta), [Bharath Reddy Baddam](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Bharath_Reddy_Baddam_bridging_mlops_gap) (New York Life) and [Naveen Edapurath Vijayan](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Naveen_Edapurath_Vijayan_mlops_scale) (AWS), who covered CI/CD for ML, monitoring and governance.

## Scale at the largest platforms

[Anjan Dash](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Anjan_Dash_mlops_trillion_parameter) (Meta) talked about operating training pipelines for trillion-parameter LLMs, where failure recovery and cost are daily concerns. [Prabhakar Singh](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Prabhakar_Singh_production_mlops_spam) (Meta) covered real-time, multimodal spam detection at billion-user scale, including sampling strategies that cut labelling cost and privacy-preserving ML. [Mohan Singh](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Mohan_Singh_building_autonomous_mlops) (Amazon) presented self-healing ML infrastructure that predicts failures and scales itself.

On agents, [Samuel Baruffi](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Samuel_Baruffi_poc_bedrock_agentcore) (AWS) addressed why so many agent projects stall at proof of concept: production needs identity, governed tool access and a runtime, which is what Amazon Bedrock AgentCore packages as managed services. [Nishanth Joseph Paulraj](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Nishanth_Joseph_Paulraj_deploying_ai_literature) (Thermo Fisher Scientific) deployed literature agents for biomedical research, and [Brian Irish](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Brian_Irish_embracing_sky_computing) (Ditto) made the case for sky computing across clouds.

## Humans, governance and security in the loop

[Tejasvi Nuthalapati](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Tejasvi_Nuthalapati_human_loop_mlops) (Amazon) argued for production patterns that keep humans in the loop to improve models rather than replace people. [Bhanu Maryala](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Bhanu_Maryala_automating_ai_ethics) (Informatica) automated data governance inside generative AI pipelines so compliance doesn't slow deployments. [Sudheer Obbu](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Sudheer_Obbu_zero_trust_mlops) (JPMorgan Chase) applied Zero Trust to ML pipelines from development to production, and [Arfi Siddik Mollashaik](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Arfi_Siddik_Mollashaik_ai_driven_cybersecurity) (Securiti) looked at threat detection and privacy with post-quantum defences in mind. Healthcare got its own governance angle from [Nikitha Edulakanti](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Nikitha_Edulakanti_mlops_scale_designing) (Fresenius Medical Care) and [Aishwarya Pai](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Aishwarya_Pai_mlops_healthcare_scaling) (Deloitte).

## Finance was the busiest track

No other domain came close. [Naresh Karri](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Naresh_Karri_production_mlops_finance) (Intuit) covered keeping fraud models alive under extreme load and drift, [Mallikarjun Ramasani](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Mallikarjun_Ramasani_operationalizing_ai_finance) (Barclays) ML-powered BI and real-time analytics, [Narasimha Rao Vanaparthi](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Narasimha_Rao_Vanaparthi_mlops_fintech_architecting) (State Street) responsible AI in regulated environments, and [Dileep Kumar Kanimetta](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Dileep_Kumar_Kanimetta_mlops_scale_operationalizing) (Microsoft) drift detection and automated governance for risk models. [Parul Purwar](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Parul_Purwar_scaling_ai_finance) (IMC Trading), [Pushpalika Chatterjee](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Pushpalika_Chatterjee_generative_ai_fintech) (Huntington National Bank) and [Naganarendar Chitturi](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Naganarendar_Chitturi_paperwork_pipeline_building) (Newrez), who built MLOps for autonomous mortgage processing, rounded it out. Insurance came via [Sameer Joshi](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Sameer_Joshi_ai_driven_insurance) (Cognizant) and [Sreelatha Pasuparthi](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Sreelatha_Pasuparthi_intelligent_error_response) (Liberty Mutual), whose ML-driven Slack integration predicts which errors matter across 17 enterprise systems.

## MLOps at the edges

Some of the most interesting constraints came from outside big tech. [Lalitha Potharalanka](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Lalitha_Potharalanka_scaling_educational_ai) (FDA) took educational AI to rural schools with poor connectivity through low-code platforms and edge inference, and [Anoosha Cherukuri](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Anoosha_Cherukuri_low_code_meets) (State of Tennessee) brought low-code automation to public sector systems. [Krishna Munnaluru](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Krishna_Munnaluru_production_ready_mlops) (Oracle) built a VoIP monitoring pipeline on Kafka, Kubeflow and MLflow, [Manoj Kumar Vunnava](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Manoj_Kumar_Vunnava_scaling_conversational_ai) (GoDaddy) scaled conversational AI in contact centres, [Muruga Angamuthu](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Muruga_Angamuthu_transforming_manufacturing_ai) (Techtronic Industries) covered predictive maintenance in manufacturing, and [Rajeev Chevuri](https://www.conf42.com/MLOps_2025_Rajeev_Chevuri_scaling_mlops_self) (Freddie Mac) built self-service ML platforms.
