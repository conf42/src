# Conf42 LLMs 2025 roundup: evals, attacks and agents in production

Conf42 Blog, 2025-03-21. https://www.conf42.com/blog/llms2025/llms-2025-roundup-evals-attacks-agents

In short: Conf42 Large Language Models 2025 in review, from evals and AI testing AI to BadGPT and prompt injection, hierarchical agents and fine-tuning economics.

In March 2025 the question at [Conf42 Large Language Models 2025](https://www.conf42.com/llms2025) was no longer whether to build on LLMs but how to know the thing you built works, and how someone might break it. The 39 talks, streamed online on March 20, 2025, split roughly into three camps: people measuring model behaviour, people attacking it, and people wiring models into agents and products. Speakers came from Arato.ai, Kusho, Palisade, Microsoft, AWS, PayPal, IBM, Lime, Warner Bros. Discovery and others.

## You can't ship what you can't measure

[Hilik Paz](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Hilik_Paz_evaluate_evals) (Arato.ai) laid out why testing changes with LLMs: well-defined outputs and errors give way to unpredictable ones, identical inputs can produce different answers, and pass or fail turns into scoring across several dimensions. Good evals, in his framing, are measurable, repeatable and actionable, and the talk covers building the test dataset that gets a prompt all the way to production.

[Sourabh Gawande](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Sourabh_Gawande_applications_practices_tradeoffs) (Kusho) brought the engineering view from 18 months of building Kusho's API-testing agent: retries and timeouts from day one, schema validation with Pydantic, Server-Sent Events for streaming, Redis-backed task queues for long jobs, and promptfoo evals in CI, which he called unit testing for prompts. [Robin Gupta](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Robin_Gupta_test_systems_process) (Testzeus) used AI to test AI, combining rule-based and model-based checks for bias, hallucinations and inconsistencies with open source tools, and [Ankush Sharma](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Ankush_Sharma_observability_models_predictions) applied SRE practices to observability for LLM systems.

## Attacks are cheaper than you think

The security track was the event's most sobering. [Dmitriy Volkov](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Dmitriy_Volkov_hacking_underrated_security) (Palisade) presented BadGPT, a fine-tuning poisoning attack that strips safety measures from a model without hurting its performance or adding token overhead. His title, "LLM hacking is underrated", was the thesis. [Jorrik Kiijnsma](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Jorrik_Kiijnsma_attacks_risks_security) (Sopra Steria) live-coded prompt injection attacks against LLM-powered web apps and the defences against them, the best kind of talk to send a frontend team.

[Kartheek Medhavi Penagamuri Shriram](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Kartheek_Medhavi_Penagamuri_Shriram_threats_risks_defenses) (Microsoft) walked through the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications, [Jose Manuel Ortega](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Jose_Manuel_Ortega_security_auditing_tools) surveyed security and auditing tools, and [Balaji Thadagam Kandavel](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Balaji_Thadagam_Kandavel_code_data_security) (Cox Automotive) made the case for deploying and fine-tuning in-house so your code and IP don't feed someone else's next model.

## Agents, RAG and the build-or-tune question

[Aleksandr Khramogin](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Aleksandr_Khramogin_sequential_agents_hierarchical) (LogicBoost) moved past sequential agent chains to hierarchical planning, with specialised agents that decompose complex tasks, built in Python with LangChain, CrewAI and OpenAI's API. [Sandeep Kumar P](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Sandeep_Kumar_P_data_insights_amazon) (AntStack) set out to show that RAG, vector databases and multi-stage flows only seem complex because they're explained badly, using Amazon Bedrock. [Manish Ballal](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Manish_Ballal_automation_process_operations) (AWS) looked at agentic automation in business process operations.

[Muddassar Sharif](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Muddassar_Sharif_fine_tuning_business) (Virtuans.ai) asked the question every team eventually faces: is fine-tuning worth the data, compute and people it needs, or is RAG the better fit? [Peter De Tender](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Peter_De_Tender_azure_models_management) (Microsoft) showed Azure AI Foundry hosting models from several vendors, [Graziano Casto](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Graziano_Casto_workloads_cloud_environments) (Mia-Platform) the gaps in running AI workloads on Kubernetes, and [Shoeb Bustani](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Shoeb_Bustani_aws_reviews_architecture) (AWS) generative AI speeding up Well-Architected reviews.

## LLMs inside real products

Some of the most concrete talks were product case studies. [Rustam Musin](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Rustam_Musin_automated_content_localization) built automated podcast localisation in Kotlin, chaining Whisper for transcription, GPT-4 for refinement and translation, and LLM-powered speech synthesis. [Kaan Tas](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Kaan_Tas_task_pricing_costs) (Lime) applied AI to pricing warehouse tasks dynamically by task density and demand. [Poornakumar Rasiraju](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Poornakumar_Rasiraju_frontend_applications_experience) (Highspot) integrated LLMs into web and mobile front ends, [Waseem Syed](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Waseem_Syed_multimodal_audio_video) used speaker diarisation and topic segmentation for audio and video, and [Rahul Basu](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Rahul_Basu_hollywood_metaverse_future) (Warner Bros. Discovery) looked at AI in entertainment.

Finance supplied a cluster of its own: payment fraud prevention ([Arpit Mittal](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Arpit_Mittal_payment_security_fraud), PayPal), human-AI reconciliation ([Naveen Gowda](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Naveen_Gowda_financial_reconciliation_compliance), Couchbase), anti-money-laundering with NICE Actimize ([Vijaya Kumar Guntumadugu](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Vijaya_Kumar_Guntumadugu_banking_compliance_money), CitiusTech), dental claims ([Pravesh Nikhare](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Pravesh_Nikhare_dental_claims_fraud), Delta Dental of California) and a CFO's perspective from [Andriy Bagryantsev](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Andriy_Bagryantsev_financial_operations_cfo).

## The humans using the models

Three talks were about people. [Huseyn Gorbani](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Huseyn_Gorbani_gap_sharp_engaged) (Rider Levett Bucknall) took on skill atrophy: the more Copilot and ChatGPT write, the more developers risk losing the ability to reason about system design. [Omer Ali Omer and Syeda Narmeen](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Omer_Ali_Omer_Syeda_Narmeen_science_evolution_practical) (IBM) looked at how a data scientist's day-to-day work changes, and [Antara Raman Sahay](https://www.conf42.com/Large_Language_Models_LLMs_2025_Antara_Raman_Sahay_making_models_process) (Helmerich and Payne) explained LLM training through four robots raised by different parents, a good first talk for anyone new to the field.
