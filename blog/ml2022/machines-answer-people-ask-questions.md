# Machines give answers, people still have to ask the questions

Conf42 Blog, 2022-05-18. https://www.conf42.com/blog/ml2022/machines-answer-people-ask-questions

In short: Jesus Saldana Gonzalez (Globant) at Conf42 Machine Learning 2022 on why machine learning displaces tasks rather than jobs, from GitHub Copilot to DALL-E

In May 2022, months before ChatGPT made the question dinner-table talk, Jesus Saldana Gonzalez, Data Scientist at [Globant](https://bit.ly/3pYFWRA), told Conf42 Machine Learning 2022 that machine learning won't put you out of a job but will change the one you have. His argument: ML takes over the repetitive tasks, people move to the work that needs curiosity, creativity and emotional intelligence, and the skill that will set professionals apart is understanding how these models think.

It's a short, optimistic opinion talk rather than a technical one, and reading it a few years later is part of the fun: GitHub Copilot and DALL-E were the impressive new examples.

## Is this really an industrial revolution?

Jesus defines an industrial revolution as a process of profound economic, social, cultural and technological transformation, and maps the drivers of the first one onto today. Population growth: industrialisation took the world from 1 billion to 2 billion people, and we are now close to 8 billion. Technological innovation: the phone in your pocket against the computers of the Moon landing. Market expansion: steamships then, online ordering now. New ways of organising work: factories then, colleagues you never see in person now (he misses the coffee breaks).

His productivity story runs in three steps: manual work, then specialisation, then machine learning, which takes over the repetitive, mechanical and tedious tasks people still do. Jobs aren't destroyed, he argues, they're displaced, and people move to tasks that better match human skills.

## What can't the algorithm take?

He names three skills. Curiosity: a model does one thing, maybe a handful, very well, and nothing else.

"Machines are great at giving answers, but it is us, the people, who will ask them the right questions," Jesus Saldana Gonzalez said at Conf42 Machine Learning 2022.

Creativity: ML is one more tool, the solution to a specific problem, and people choose when to use it. Emotional intelligence: a computer vision model might read your facial expression, but when you call your bank or your hospital you still expect a human voice, and you notice when it's a robot. He quotes Lisa Messenger, CEO of Collective Hub, on business coming down to humanising things.

## Copilot needs a programmer, DALL-E needs an artist

The examples are the tools of the moment. GitHub Copilot can implement a method or a data structure from a comment, which looks like it could replace a programmer, but it needs someone to drive it, like a hammer needs an arm. And try having a customer who doesn't know what they want explain it to a computer. His prediction is that such tools save experienced programmers time and make it easier for newcomers to study complex ideas without spending as much time implementing them.

On media, he points to models that remove objects or watermarks from video, stabilise footage, add frames, restore old photographs and isolate a single instrument in a recording. Then generation: DALL-E producing an astronaut playing basketball with cats in space in a minimalist style, or a bowl of soup that is a portal to another dimension in the style of Basquiat. The model needs an input, so it needs a creative person behind it, and only someone who knows Basquiat's style thinks to ask for it.

## Augmented intelligence, or the calculator argument

His closing concept is augmented intelligence: using AI to augment your own capabilities. The analogy is the calculator. Counting on your fingers is safe and slow; the first time you used a calculator, typing the numbers took longer than the sum. Today ML suggests an expert mathematician typing code, and that profile stays necessary, but the algorithms will be packaged and polished until using them is like learning any new tool. The challenge then is understanding how the models think and how to communicate with them to get the result you want.
