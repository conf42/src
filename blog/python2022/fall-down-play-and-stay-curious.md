# To learn like a child, get good at falling down

Conf42 Blog, 2022-01-26. https://www.conf42.com/blog/python2022/fall-down-play-and-stay-curious

In short: Rain Leander (Cockroach Labs) at Conf42 Python 2022 on regressing to a child's mindset of falling, bravery, play and curiosity to grow as an engineer

Adults learn new tech slowly for a reason that has nothing to do with brains: somebody once laughed when we fell off the bike. At Conf42 Python 2022, Rain Leander, Developer Advocate at Cockroach Labs, gave a short, warm talk on regressing to make progress: getting back in touch with your inner child, who already knows how to fall, be brave, play and explore. Her claim is that if you do those four things, the growth follows on its own.

This is a few-minutes pep talk, not a technical session. There is no Python and no database in it; it is the kind of talk you put on before starting something new and slightly scary.

## Why is learning harder as an adult?

Her opening example is riding a bike. If you learn as a child it is easy; learn later and it is much harder, and she argues the reason is an aversion to failure we pick up along the way. We want to be instantly the best at anything new. She has bad news about that: you are not a secret ninja without ninja classes, and you will not win the Tour de France if you never learned to ride. You will fall down, mess up and make mistakes, and that is fine, as long as you get back up.

## Bravery is learned, not born

Rain is honest that this is where the child analogy creaks: with a seven-year-old and a pair of four-year-olds at home, she does not rate children as especially brave. Bravery is developed. "Being brave is not a lack of fear. It's moving forward despite that fear," Rain Leander said at Conf42 Python 2022.

Her baby steps will sound familiar to anyone who has broken down an epic into tickets: admit the fear, do it anyway, and remember that every big scary monster or dragon or problem can be split into bite-sized pieces. Acknowledge the fear, take a few minutes to make a plan, then run the plan.

## Play is not optional

For skeptics she points to the National Institute for Play, which collects research on play's effect on physical and mental health, relationships, learning and innovation. Children get play built into the day. Adults get less recess, no more breaks and, in her words, fewer naps, which she has strong opinions about. She cannot tell you how to play, since that depends entirely on what you enjoy, but she can tell you to put it on your calendar and to find a new fun thing when the old one stops being fun.

## Curiosity is the engine

People are explorers from birth, she says, and to stop exploring is to stop being true to yourself. Curiosity drives exploration, and also networking, problem solving and spotting opportunities. Her explorer's mindset: be curious, listen a lot, ask a lot of questions, treat learning as fun, keep a beginner's mind, and listen some more.

The closing twist is that you are already the unicorn of the abstract. The label just means unique, and nobody else has your mix of experience, background, ups and downs.

## Who should watch

Anyone stuck at the start of learning a new stack, mentors who need words for why juniors should be allowed to fail, and people who want a quick lift between denser talks. If you want CockroachDB internals, look elsewhere; the talk mentions neither the product nor Python.
