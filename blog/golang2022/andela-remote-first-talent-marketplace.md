# Andela's pitch to engineers: 175,000 technologists, 96% placed, remote first

Conf42 Blog, 2022-03-30. https://www.conf42.com/blog/golang2022/andela-remote-first-talent-marketplace

In short: Agnes Muthoni (Andela) at Conf42 Golang 2022 on how Andela screens, matches and places remote engineers from 90+ countries with global companies

Agnes Muthoni, Director of Talent Partnerships at Andela, gave Conf42 Golang 2022 a short, direct pitch: Andela is a global talent marketplace that sources, screens and places remote software engineers with companies such as GitHub, Goldman Sachs and Cloudflare, and by her figures it has 175,000 technologists in its community and a 96% successful placement rate.

Be clear about what this is. It is a recruiting talk from the sponsor, a few minutes long, with no Go in it. If you are an engineer weighing remote work for an international company, it answers the practical questions about how the model works.

## How does the Andela model work?

Muthoni describes it as a pipeline with five steps: source talent, screen it, onboard it onto the talent network, match engineers against a specific client's requirements, and place them on the engagement once they meet the client's criteria. The screening runs on what she calls a data-driven process that has gone through hundreds of thousands of applications. The target is specific: experienced software engineers who can deliver against a client's product roadmap, not juniors to be trained on the job. Clients get to scale an engineering team quickly; engineers get a placement that has already been checked against what the client actually needs.

The company started by finding engineers in emerging markets (its first remote regions were Egypt and Ghana) and expanded from there. Engineers in the network now come from more than 90 countries on six continents; the opening reel names São Paulo, Egypt, Lagos and Warsaw.

## Why does she think remote is the default now?

Her argument is that Andela bet on a distributed workforce from day one, and the rest of the industry caught up over the last couple of years. The idea is to level the field both ways: companies hire on talent regardless of location, and engineers pick challenging work with global companies without moving.

"Remote first is changing the way we work and opening up opportunities for developers in emerging markets to enter the global talent marketplace," Agnes Muthoni said at Conf42 Golang 2022.

She cites Andela's own developer report: more than 80% of developers are keen on working remotely.

## What do engineers get out of it?

She lists long-term engagements, a global community of engineers, career coaching, educational advancement and flexibility over where you live and work. The numbers she quotes come from a recent Andela survey of its engineers:

- 74% reported an improvement in quality of life.
- 65% said they were better able to save.
- 81% of those with more than a year of tenure reported higher take-home pay.

The Andela Learning Community, she says, has taken thousands of engineers through learning journeys, with in-demand technology courses from education partners including Google, Microsoft, Facebook and Salesforce, plus links to job boards.

On the client side, she pitches distributed hiring as a way for companies to build more diverse teams, which many corporate leaders name as a priority.

## Who should watch

Engineers outside the big tech hubs who want to work for international companies without relocating, and hiring managers curious how a vetted remote talent marketplace pitches itself. Everyone else at a Go conference can skip to the technical talks.

What the talk leaves out is worth knowing too: it doesn't describe what the screening and assessments involve, how matching decisions are made, or what the contract terms look like. The survey figures are Andela's own. If you are seriously considering it, those are the questions to bring to a first call.
