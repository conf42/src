# Anti-Entropy Mechanisms for Single-Digit Minute Latency in Cloud-Scale Security Platforms

A talk at [Conf42 DevSecOps 2026](https://www.conf42.com/devsecops2026) by [Rahul Kizhakkepachilamakol](https://www.conf42.com/speaker_Rahul_Kizhakkepachilamakol) (Principal Software Engineer @ Amazon Web Services). ## Abstract

Cloud providers now generate continuous streams of vulnerability findings across globally distributed infrastructure, and every minute of delay in delivering a finding widens the window of exposure for the government agencies, financial institutions, and healthcare systems that depend on that infrastructure. Distributed platforms responsible for this delivery face a classic tension, formalized by the CAP theorem, between consistency, availability, and partition tolerance, making single-digit-minute delivery difficult to reconcile with correctness at cloud scale. This talk presents an architecture that resolves that tension by adapting anti-entropy patterns from distributed database research, including Merkle-tree comparison, read-repair, and hinted handoff, into a security-specific reconciliation layer. Findings are enriched with severity and asset context, then reconciled through priority-aware, attribute-level hashing that captures not just missing data but silent corruption of existing findings. Paired with a streaming-first ingestion layer, this anti-entropy backstop delivers the vast majority of findings within seconds and closes the remaining gap within single-digit minutes, all while preserving an audit-compliant record of every repair. Attendees, including platform architects, security engineers, and engineering leaders, will leave with concrete, transferable patterns for designing distributed systems that must reconcile speed, correctness, and scale under real-world failure conditions.
