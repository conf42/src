# Beyond Killing Pods: Chaos Engineering for Kubernetes and Microservices

A talk at [Conf42 Chaos Engineering 2026](https://www.conf42.com/chaos2026) by [Madhumanti Majumdar](https://www.conf42.com/speaker_Madhumanti_Majumdar) (Senior Software Engineer @ Walmart). Page: https://www.conf42.com/Chaos_Engineering_2026_Madhumanti_Majumdar_kubernetes_microservices_pods

## Abstract

Deleting a Kubernetes pod is easy. Understanding whether your distributed system can survive a real production failure is much harder.  Modern microservices depend on complex interactions between Kubernetes, service meshes, messaging systems, databases, and external networks. While infrastructure may recover automatically, application-level failures can still cascade through retries, timeouts, overloaded dependencies, and unexpected resource contention.  In this session, I'll explore how SRE teams can move beyond basic fault injection and design meaningful chaos experiments that test actual system resilience.  Drawing on my experience engineering and operating large-scale distributed platforms, I'll discuss how to identify critical failure scenarios, define steady-state behavior, measure blast radius, and use observability to understand how systems respond under stress.  We'll examine practical scenarios involving Kubernetes workloads, network disruptions, Kafka dependencies, and service-to-service communication, with an emphasis on making experiments safe, measurable, and actionable.  The goal is to help engineers build confidence in their recovery mechanisms before real incidents put them to the test.  Key takeaways - Design chaos experiments around realistic production failure modes. - Define measurable steady-state hypotheses and recovery criteria. - Understand cascading failures across Kubernetes, service meshes, and messaging systems. - Use observability to measure impact and validate recovery. - Turn experiment findings into concrete reliability improvements.  Intended audience SREs, platform engineers, DevOps engineers, software architects, and engineering leaders.
