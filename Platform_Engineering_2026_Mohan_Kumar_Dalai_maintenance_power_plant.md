# AI Maintenance Platforms for Reliable Power Plant Operations

A talk at [Conf42 Platform Engineering 2026](https://www.conf42.com/platform2026) by [Mohan Kumar Dalai](https://www.conf42.com/speaker_Mohan_Kumar_Dalai) (staff software engineer @ GEVERNOVA). Page: https://www.conf42.com/Platform_Engineering_2026_Mohan_Kumar_Dalai_maintenance_power_plant

Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lX1GI6cLX2k

## Abstract

Power plants depend on the continuous performance of critical assets such as turbines, generators, boilers, and transformers, where a single forced outage can affect operational cost, grid reliability, and safety. However, traditional reactive and time-based maintenance approaches often fall short because they do not fully account for real operating conditions, asset degradation patterns, and complex system interactions. This session presents AI-driven maintenance as a practical platform engineering challenge for modern power plant operations. Instead of treating predictive maintenance as isolated models or standalone analytics, the talk explains how AI capabilities can be built into scalable reliability platforms that connect plant data, diagnostics, workflows, observability, and decision support. The session examines how machine learning, deep learning, expert systems, and natural language processing can be applied across major plant systems. Examples include vibration and bearing diagnostics for rotating machinery, combustion and heat transfer modeling for boilers and HRSGs, dissolved gas analysis for transformers, and AI-assisted alarm filtering to reduce operator overload. The discussion also covers the platform design choices required to make these capabilities usable in real operations, including data pipeline reliability, legacy system integration, explainability, cybersecurity controls, governance, and maintainable workflows. Attendees will gain practical insight into what it takes to move from reactive maintenance and disconnected analytics toward a more integrated AI-enabled asset reliability platform. The talk shares a structured five-step implementation framework, highlights adoption barriers such as data quality gaps and integration complexity, and concludes with future-facing capabilities such as digital twins, autonomous inspection robotics, and enterprise-wide asset analytics. The goal is to provide engineers, architects, and platform practitioners with clear, practical takeaways for building and operating AI-driven maintenance systems that improve reliability, reduce maintenance friction, and support safer, more sustainable power generation.
